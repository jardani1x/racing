# Visual reference brief

## What to carry forward from the supplied images

### Vehicle presentation

- low, close camera height that emphasizes wheel, diffuser, splitter, and aero scale;
- front/rear three-quarter compositions rather than flat side views;
- clear material separation among paint, carbon, glass, rubber, metal, lights, and decals;
- realistic body reflections and highlight roll-off;
- visible tire deformation/contact impression, suspension compression, steering angle, brake/wheel depth, and chassis attitude;
- restrained but effective motion blur and background parallax.

### Track presentation

- substantial elevation and long vanishing lines;
- believable circuit width, camber, kerb construction, runoff, walls, fencing, service roads, and vegetation;
- dense but coherent trackside objects;
- wear, patching, drainage, rubber, grime, and variation at multiple scales;
- lighting and atmosphere that preserve distance and readable racing surfaces.

### Race identity

- professional motorsport visual language;
- functional safety infrastructure;
- clean readable HUD rather than an arcade overlay;
- high-quality original liveries and signs until real-world licenses exist.

## What not to copy

- vehicle meshes, exact silhouettes, branded badges, model names, liveries, sponsor marks, or decals;
- exact real-circuit geometry, signage, structures, names, or trademarks;
- Gran Turismo interface, logos, screenshots, camera cuts, or proprietary assets;
- copyrighted images/textures as source maps.

## Benchmark shots to create

1. Rear three-quarter chase view on a steep descent or crest.
2. Low front three-quarter trackside view under load at corner exit.
3. Long-lens uphill straight with multiple depth layers.
4. Cockpit view approaching braking markers.
5. Static material turntable for car paint/carbon/glass/rubber.
6. Track-surface macro shot and a long-distance environment shot.

Lock these cameras and use them for screenshot regression and human art review.
